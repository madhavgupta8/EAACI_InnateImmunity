M1 Foundation

  • Innate receptors: germline-encoded, nonclonal, <100 types; memory limited. A64,4
  • Phases: immediate innate 0–4 h, early induced 4–96 h, adaptive >96 h. J37
  • Paneth cells make α-defensins. LL-37 (cathelicidin) neutralizes LPS. A80
  • TNF, IL-1, IL-6 → fever, leukocytosis, acute-phase proteins; CRP can rise 1000-fold. A96,97,88
  • Epithelial IL-25, IL-33, TSLP drive ILC2s, Th2 cells, mast cells; IL-33 is an alarmin. A94
  • Trap: lysozyme works better on Gram-positive bacteria (LPS covers Gram-negative peptidoglycan). J45
  • Trap: anti-TNF gave no benefit in sepsis trials and can reactivate TB. A97 J109

M2 Cells

  • Neutrophils: most abundant WBC, arrive first; azurophilic granules hold MPO and defensins. A14,94
  • Most tissue macrophages are fetal-derived and self-renew (Janeway: from monocytes). A15 J76
  • pDC (TLR7, TLR9) = major type I IFN source; cDC1 cross-presents to CD8+ T cells. A23,22,21-23
  • NK cells: CD56+ CD3−; low MHC-I ("missing self") removes KIR/NKG2A inhibition; CD16 → ADCC. A82,85,84
  • NK deficiency (GATA2, CD16, CHS) → severe herpesvirus and papillomavirus infections. A488
  • C3a, C5a degranulate mast cells; MRGPRX2 → IgE-independent drug reactions. A470
  • ILC2 (GATA3; IL-5, IL-13) responds to IL-25, IL-33, TSLP. A81
  • Trap: tryptase, not histamine, marks mast cell activation (all mast cells, no other cell). A471
  • Trap: eosinophil FcεRI is low and lacks a signalling chain; IL-5 is the key cytokine. A473

M3 PRRs & signalling

  • Surface TLR1/2/4/5/6 see wall products; endosomal TLR3/7/8/9 see nucleic acids. A69
  • LPS → TLR4 (+MD2, CD14); flagellin → TLR5; dsRNA → TLR3; CpG DNA → TLR9. A67,69
  • MyD88/IRAK4 deficiency → early invasive pneumococcal disease, no fever ("cold"). A489 J535
  • TLR3, UNC93B, TRIF defects → herpes simplex encephalitis. A489
  • NLRP3–ASC–caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β/IL-18; gasdermin D pores → pyroptosis. A72,74
  • NLRP3 gain of function → CAPS (FCAS: cold-induced; Muckle-Wells: deafness); IL-1 blockade works. A75,74 J537
  • Trap: TLR3 is the only TLR without MyD88; TLR4 uses MyD88 and TRIF. A69
  • Trap: weak NOD2 variants → Crohn's; NOD2 gain of function → Blau syndrome. A72

M4 Phagocytosis & defects

  • Recruitment: selectin rolling (sialyl Lewis X) → chemokine integrin activation → ICAM-1/VCAM-1 arrest → transmigration. A49,45
  • CXCL8 (CXCR1/2) → neutrophils; CCL2 (CCR2) → monocytes. A50
  • CGD: about 2/3 X-linked gp91; catalase+ Staphylococcus; Aspergillus = leading cause of death; granulomas. A486,487
  • LAD-1 (CD18): leukocytosis, no pus, poor wound healing. LAD-2: no sialyl Lewis X, Bombay group. LAD-3 (KINDLIN-3): bleeding. A488
  • CHS (LYST): partial albinism, giant lysosomes, NK defect, HLH. A488
  • MSMD (IL-12p40, IL-12Rβ1, IFN-γR, STAT1, NEMO): mycobacteria, Salmonella, BCG spread. A498
  • Trap: NADPH oxidase makes superoxide; MPO makes hypohalous acid. MPO deficiency is CGD-like but milder. A96 J536

M5 Complement

  • C3 cleavage is central. C3 convertases: C4b2a (classical, lectin), C3bBb (alternative). A301,305 J54
  • IgM fixes complement better than IgG; IgG1, IgG3 best; IgG4 none. A305
  • Factor D circulates active; properdin stabilizes C3bBb. J61 A302
  • C5a: most potent anaphylatoxin; degranulates mast cells without IgE. A314,470
  • CR2 (CD21) binds C3d, is the EBV receptor and helps B-cell activation. A308,310,314
  • Trap: factor H regulates only the alternative pathway; C4BP regulates C4b. A311,310
  • Trap: CD59 blocks C9 at C5b-8; DAF dissociates the C3 convertases. A312,310

M6 Deficiencies & HAE

  • C1q/C2/C4 → SLE (>50%); C2 deficiency is the most common. A315
  • C3 or factor I deficiency → pyogenic infections. A315
  • C5–C9, properdin (X-linked), factor D → Neisseria. A315 J532
  • aHUS: factor H, MCP. PNH: somatic PIG-A → no CD59, DAF. A315,312
  • HAE-1: low C1-INH function and protein. HAE-2: protein normal/high, function low. C4 usually low. GL5,6
  • AAE-C1-INH: later onset, lymphoma/MGUS, C1q low in 75%. GL7
  • ACE inhibitors: avoid in HAE; ACEI-AE has normal C1-INH. GL14,6
  • Trap: C3 and CH50 are normal in HAE; C4 is unreliable before 12 months. GL6-7,11
  • Trap: HAE-1/2 does not respond to antihistamines, steroids or adrenaline; wheals do not exclude HAE. GL6,7
2017 guideline (volatile — check current): treat attacks early with C1-INH, ecallantide or icatibant; no tranexamic acid or androgens on demand. LTP: C1-INH first line, androgens second. GL7,7-8,8,9-10,10
