M1 Foundation

  • Innate receptors: germline-encoded, nonclonal, <100 types; memory limited. {{M1-F023,M1-F024,M1-F008}}
  • Phases: immediate innate 0–4 h, early induced 4–96 h, adaptive >96 h. {{M1-F060}}
  • Paneth cells make α-defensins. LL-37 (cathelicidin) neutralizes LPS. {{M1-F045,M1-F049}}
  • TNF, IL-1, IL-6 → fever, leukocytosis, acute-phase proteins; CRP can rise 1000-fold. {{M1-F089,M1-F095,M1-F096,M1-F097}}
  • Epithelial IL-25, IL-33, TSLP drive ILC2s, Th2 cells, mast cells; IL-33 is an alarmin. {{M1-F087}}
  • Trap: lysozyme works better on Gram-positive bacteria (LPS covers Gram-negative peptidoglycan). {{M1-F040}}
  • Trap: anti-TNF gave no benefit in sepsis trials and can reactivate TB. {{M1-F109,M1-F111}}

M2 Cells

  • Neutrophils: most abundant WBC, arrive first; azurophilic granules hold MPO and defensins. {{M2-F013,M1-F083,M2-F016}}
  • Most tissue macrophages are fetal-derived and self-renew (Janeway: from monocytes). {{M2-F022,M2-F024}}
  • pDC (TLR7, TLR9) = major type I IFN source; cDC1 cross-presents to CD8+ T cells. {{M2-F036,M2-F037,M2-F035}}
  • NK cells: CD56+ CD3−; low MHC-I ("missing self") removes KIR/NKG2A inhibition; CD16 → ADCC. {{M2-F043,M2-F053,M2-F054,M2-F052}}
  • NK deficiency (GATA2, CD16, CHS) → severe herpesvirus and papillomavirus infections. {{M2-F059,M2-F060,M2-F061,M2-F062}}
  • C3a, C5a degranulate mast cells; MRGPRX2 → IgE-independent drug reactions. {{M2-F074,M2-F075}}
  • ILC2 (GATA3; IL-5, IL-13) responds to IL-25, IL-33, TSLP. {{M2-F094,M2-F095}}
  • Trap: tryptase, not histamine, marks mast cell activation (all mast cells, no other cell). {{M2-F077}}
  • Trap: eosinophil FcεRI is low and lacks a signalling chain; IL-5 is the key cytokine. {{M2-F090,M2-F089}}

M3 PRRs & signalling

  • Surface TLR1/2/4/5/6 see wall products; endosomal TLR3/7/8/9 see nucleic acids. {{M3-F009,M3-F010}}
  • LPS → TLR4 (+MD2, CD14); flagellin → TLR5; dsRNA → TLR3; CpG DNA → TLR9. {{M3-F012,M3-F020,M3-F021,M3-F013,M3-F014,M3-F015}}
  • MyD88/IRAK4 deficiency → early invasive pneumococcal disease, no fever ("cold"). {{M3-F036,M3-F037,M3-F038}}
  • TLR3, UNC93B, TRIF defects → herpes simplex encephalitis. {{M3-F040,M3-F041,M3-F042}}
  • NLRP3–ASC–caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β/IL-18; gasdermin D pores → pyroptosis. {{M3-F052,M3-F053,M3-F054,M3-F059}}
  • NLRP3 gain of function → CAPS (FCAS: cold-induced; Muckle-Wells: deafness); IL-1 blockade works. {{M3-F067,M3-F068,M3-F069,M3-F066}}
  • Trap: TLR3 is the only TLR without MyD88; TLR4 uses MyD88 and TRIF. {{M3-F025}}
  • Trap: weak NOD2 variants → Crohn's; NOD2 gain of function → Blau syndrome. {{M3-F048,M3-F049}}

M4 Phagocytosis & defects

  • Recruitment: selectin rolling (sialyl Lewis X) → chemokine integrin activation → ICAM-1/VCAM-1 arrest → transmigration. {{M4-F016,M4-F006,M4-F017}}
  • CXCL8 (CXCR1/2) → neutrophils; CCL2 (CCR2) → monocytes. {{M4-F024,M4-F025}}
  • CGD: about 2/3 X-linked gp91; catalase+ Staphylococcus; Aspergillus = leading cause of death; granulomas. {{M4-F073,M4-F077,M4-F076,M4-F078}}
  • LAD-1 (CD18): leukocytosis, no pus, poor wound healing. LAD-2: no sialyl Lewis X, Bombay group. LAD-3 (KINDLIN-3): bleeding. {{M4-F085,M4-F086,M4-F084,M4-F088,M4-F089,M4-F090,M4-F091}}
  • CHS (LYST): partial albinism, giant lysosomes, NK defect, HLH. {{M4-F093,M4-F094,M4-F095,M4-F096}}
  • MSMD (IL-12p40, IL-12Rβ1, IFN-γR, STAT1, NEMO): mycobacteria, Salmonella, BCG spread. {{M4-F104,M4-F105}}
  • Trap: NADPH oxidase makes superoxide; MPO makes hypohalous acid. MPO deficiency is CGD-like but milder. {{M4-F051,M4-F052,M4-F082}}

M5 Complement

  • C3 cleavage is central. C3 convertases: C4b2a (classical, lectin), C3bBb (alternative). {{M5-F010,M5-F022,M5-F029,M5-F032}}
  • IgM fixes complement better than IgG; IgG1, IgG3 best; IgG4 none. {{M5-F018,M5-F014,M5-F015}}
  • Factor D circulates active; properdin stabilizes C3bBb. {{M5-F034,M5-F035}}
  • C5a: most potent anaphylatoxin; degranulates mast cells without IgE. {{M5-F054,M5-F057}}
  • CR2 (CD21) binds C3d, is the EBV receptor and helps B-cell activation. {{M5-F062,M5-F063,M5-F064}}
  • Trap: factor H regulates only the alternative pathway; C4BP regulates C4b. {{M5-F074,M5-F076}}
  • Trap: CD59 blocks C9 at C5b-8; DAF dissociates the C3 convertases. {{M5-F083,M5-F081}}

M6 Deficiencies & HAE

  • C1q/C2/C4 → SLE (>50%); C2 deficiency is the most common. {{M6-F002,M6-F001}}
  • C3 or factor I deficiency → pyogenic infections. {{M6-F008,M6-F021}}
  • C5–C9, properdin (X-linked), factor D → Neisseria. {{M6-F017,M6-F014,M6-F015}}
  • aHUS: factor H, MCP. PNH: somatic PIG-A → no CD59, DAF. {{M6-F026,M6-F030,M6-F032}}
  • HAE-1: low C1-INH function and protein. HAE-2: protein normal/high, function low. C4 usually low. {{M6-F040,M6-F041,M6-F060}}
  • AAE-C1-INH: later onset, lymphoma/MGUS, C1q low in 75%. {{M6-F063,M6-F065}}
  • ACE inhibitors: avoid in HAE; ACEI-AE has normal C1-INH. {{M6-F075,M6-F044}}
  • Trap: C3 and CH50 are normal in HAE; C4 is unreliable before 12 months. {{M6-F061,M6-F072}}
  • Trap: HAE-1/2 does not respond to antihistamines, steroids or adrenaline; wheals do not exclude HAE. {{M6-F057,M6-F067}}
2017 guideline (volatile — check current): treat attacks early with C1-INH, ecallantide or icatibant; no tranexamic acid or androgens on demand. LTP: C1-INH first line, androgens second. {{M6-F077,M6-F078,M6-F079,M6-F091,M6-F092}}
